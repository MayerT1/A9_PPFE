## What it is

A web portal that runs a weather foundation model on NOAA HRRR forecasts to produce **finer-scale (downscaled) rainfall and temperature**. It then turns that prediction into **flood decision support**: where water is likely to collect, which roads and facilities are exposed, and how to route around them. The portal covers areas in **New Jersey** and **Iowa**.

## Who it is for

- Emergency managers, public works and stormwater staff.
- Planners and researchers who need a quick, map-based read of an HRRR forecast at neighbourhood scale, with a shareable report.

## What you can do

| Step | In the portal |
|---|---|
| Pick an area | Choose a New Jersey or Iowa AOI and zoom to it. |
| Pick a forecast | Choose the HRRR date, model cycle (UTC) and forecast hour. |
| Run | The portal fetches HRRR, stages it on the AOI grid and runs the model. |
| Read the map | Downscaled rain, temperature, runoff accumulation and the flood hazard index appear over OSM. FEMA flood zones, rivers and critical infrastructure can be added. Click anywhere for values. |
| Route | Compare the fastest road route with a hazard-aware route that avoids risky roads. Trace where runoff from any point flows downstream. |
| Annotate | Draw points, lines and areas with notes; import or export GeoJSON. |
| Save and report | Log in to save workspaces. Export a PDF with the map, prediction summary, exposed facilities, routes and annotations. |

## How it works

1. **Fetch HRRR.** The portal downloads only the needed HRRR fields from NOAA's archive on AWS (or from local files) and regrids them to a regular ~3 km grid over the AOI.
2. **Run the model.** The foundation model downscales ×4 (to ~750 m). The platform tiles large areas with overlap so tile seams don't show.
3. **Route the water.** A terrain model is depression-filled. Every cell gets a steepest-descent (D8) flow direction, and predicted rain is accumulated downstream into a runoff volume.
4. **Score hazard.** Rain intensity, upstream runoff and FEMA Special Flood Hazard Areas combine into a 0–1 index.
5. **Find exposure.** Hospitals, fire and police stations, schools, shelters, substations and water facilities are checked against the hazard index.
6. **Serve.** Layers are drawn on the map, and point queries, routing and reports run on demand.

## Flood hazard index

`hazard = (0.45·rain + 0.40·flow·√(rain+0.05) + 0.15·FEMA) / weight sum`, clipped to 0–1. The terms are:

- **rain**: 1-hour rain ÷ 25 mm
- **flow**: log upstream runoff ÷ log(2×10⁶ m³)
- **FEMA**: 1 inside a Special Flood Hazard Area

| Class | Index |
|---|---|
| Low | < 0.35 |
| Moderate | 0.35–0.55 |
| High | 0.55–0.75 |
| Severe | ≥ 0.75 |

Weights and thresholds are **provisional** and pending domain review.

## Routing

- **Road route.** OSM roads within the trip area become a graph. The fastest route ignores the forecast. The hazard-aware route multiplies travel time by (1 + 4·h²) and closes roads where hazard h is at or above the user's threshold (default 0.70). The portal reports the detour cost.
- **Flow path.** Follows the D8 steepest-descent direction from a clicked point to the AOI edge. The portal reports length, peak hazard along the path and runoff volume at the end.

## Limits and cautions

- **Not for operational use yet.** The model is an untrained placeholder used to test the platform.
- **Coarse grids.** HRRR is ~3 km. Downscaled output is ~750 m, and flow routing runs on an analysis grid of at most 200k cells. Neither resolves individual streets or culverts.
- **Simple hydrology.** No infiltration model (a fixed runoff coefficient of 0.5), no drainage infrastructure and no tides or storm surge.
- **Statewide FEMA.** For statewide AOIs, FEMA zones are shown on the map but not included in the hazard index.
- **External services.** If OSM, FEMA or DEM services are unreachable, the portal falls back to labelled stand-ins and says so in the run panel.
- **Wind direction.** HRRR winds are on the model grid, not rotated to true north.
